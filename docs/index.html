What it is

spigot, turned from one seller into a network.

spigot sells one file by the byte, from one node. shoal is a swarm of them: no node holds the whole file, or needs to — a phone can carry a few pieces of what's popular right now. The bytes move peer-to-peer over the ordinary internet, the same way a torrent or a video call does. Only the money touches the chain, and it's tiny.

Which makes it, plainly, BitTorrent with the one thing BitTorrent never had: a way to pay the seeders, per piece, as the bytes arrive. Torrents die when the seeders lose interest, because seeding is charity. Here it's a job.

How a download works

Pull every chunk from whoever's fastest. Verify it. Pay them.

  1. The file is content-addressed into a manifest — an ordered list of chunk hashes. The manifest names the file; each hash names a chunk.
  2. A tracker says who holds what — which providers have which chunks. (A central tracker for now, exactly as BitTorrent shipped before the DHT.)
  3. Chunks are pulled in parallel and checked against the manifest before anything is believed or paid. A wrong chunk hashes wrong and is refused — no trust required.
  4. Each provider is paid for what it served and that verified — a fraction of a cent per chunk, on Kaspa. A junk-server earns nothing and is routed around; the file still completes.
CAN'T BE PAID JUNK

A wrong chunk never bills

It doesn't match the hash you had before you asked, so it's refused, not paid for. The manifest is the referee — no agreement or trust needed for delivery.

CAN'T BE OVERBILLED

The amount is fixed in advance

A chunk's price is its size from the manifest, which you held before the request. A provider can't inflate it. Stop partway and you've paid for exactly what arrived, to the byte.

Why Kaspa — the reason it can exist

It pays its participants per chunk, as they work. Only a real-time chain allows that.

A swarm makes thousands of tiny payments a second — one per chunk, per consumer, per provider. That's impossible if settling takes minutes and costs more than the payment. It's why decentralized CDNs today batch payments or lean on a token, quietly putting a trusted middle back.

Real-time decentralization is Kaspa's own doctrine — and shoal is it, made physical.

Kaspa states its purpose as real-time decentralization: censorship-resistance and permissionless settlement in real time, not eventually, on a fair-launched proof-of-work chain. A delivery swarm that pays as it serves is exactly that — the money keeps pace with the bytes because Kaspa confirms in about a second at up to ten blocks a second. The swarm isn't a clever use of a fast chain; it's the thing the fast chain is for.

What's built — running and tested

The swarm runs end to end. Ten tests hold it still.

In-process, on the bench: a file reassembles byte-identical from three providers pulled at once, each paid for its share; a fourth provider serving corrupted bytes is caught by the manifest, paid nothing, and routed around; and stopping partway pays only for what arrived. On-chain settlement reuses the same rail spigot proved on testnet — one kaspa-x402 channel per provider, one voucher for its earned total. The trust layer that catches and punishes a repeat offender is quorum.

The honest hard parts

Named, not faked.

Getting bytes between phones
Carrier-grade NAT hides mobile devices, so two phones can't just connect. This needs relays or hole-punching, and it's the single biggest engineering risk. PCs, routers and relays likely carry the early network; phones start as consumers and Wi-Fi cache-holders.
A real DHT
The central tracker is the honest v1, but it's a single list. Replacing it with a distributed hash table — so there's no central list at all — is a known later step.
Supply needs demand
Nodes won't run for pennies with nobody buying. One real buyer — a single site or app delivering through it — matters more than a thousand idle nodes.
It challenges the CDN business
Cloudflare, Akamai, AWS own the caches near you today. The nearer rivals are other decentralized-delivery projects (Meson, Filecoin Saturn, Theta) — whose chains can't settle per chunk in real time, which is the wedge.
